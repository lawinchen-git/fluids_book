Density

Density is a concentration of mass ⇔\Leftrightarrow⇔ a mass per unit volume.

With the standard symbols:

Definition: Density

The density of a fluid is its mass per unit volume,

ρ=mV. \displaystyle\rho = \frac{m}{V}.ρ=Vm​.

It is always a good plan to stick with SI units. This makes it easier to spot mistakes and work out units you are not sure about.

Example 1 (Density)

Calculate the density of 1.2 m31.2\,\mathsf{m}^31.2m3 water with mass 1200 kg1200\,\mathsf{kg}1200kg.

Solution

To find the density we need the volume of a known mass of fluid, or the mass of a known volume of fluid.

A fluid density ρ=m/V\rho=m/Vρ=m/V, where mmm is the mass and VVV the volume of the fluid respectively. Here, the water has mass m=1200 kgm=1200\,\mathsf{kg}m=1200kg and volume V=1.2 m3V=1.2\,\mathsf{m}^3V=1.2m3. Hence,

ρ=12001.2 kg/m3\rho=\frac{1200}{1.2}\,\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}^3ρ=1.21200​kg/m3
(1)

  ⟹  ρ=1000 kg/m3‾.\implies \underline{\rho = 1000\,\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}^3}.⟹ρ=1000kg/m3​.
(2)

Sometimes you see the density of a fluid described relative to the density of water. This is the relative density; the ratio of a fluid density to the density of water, with (ρw=1000 kg/m3\rho_w=1000\,\mathsf{kg/m}^3ρw​=1000kg/m3).

An alternative formulation you often see is the inverse of density, or specific volume. The word specific means “per unit mass”, so specific volume is the “volume per unit mass”, which is 1/ρ1/\rho1/ρ.

Definition: Specific volume

The specific volume of a fluid is its volume per unit mass; the inverse of its density,

v=Vm=1ρ. \displaystyle \mathcal{v} =\frac{V}{m}= \frac{1}{\rho}.v=mV​=ρ1​.

Example 2 (Specific volume)

A sample of water has a mass of 4.0 kg4.0\,\mathsf{kg}4.0kg and occupies a volume of 0.0040 m30.0040\,\mathsf{m}^30.0040m3. Calculate the specific volume of the water.

Solution

The specific volume of the water v=V/m\mathcal{v}=V/mv=V/m (=1/ρ=1/\rho=1/ρ) with mass m=4.0 kgm=4.0\,\mathsf{kg}m=4.0kg and volume V=0.0040 m3V=0.0040\,\mathsf{m}^3V=0.0040m3 respectively. Hence,

v=0.00404 m3/kg\mathcal{v}=\frac{0.0040}{4}\,\mathsf{m}^3/\mathsf{kg}v=40.0040​m3/kg
(3)

Bulk density refers to the average density of an inhomogeneous substance i.e.~the total mass per unit of mixture volume.

Example 3 (Bulk density)

A tank contains a water–solid suspension with a total mass of 120 kg120\,\mathsf{kg}120kg. The suspension occupies 0.100 m30.100\,\mathsf{m}^30.100m3. Calculate the bulk density of the suspension.

Solution

The bulk density of the suspension ρ=m/V\rho=m/Vρ=m/V, where the total mass m=120 kgm=120\,\mathsf{kg}m=120kg and total volume V=0.100 m3V=0.100\,\mathsf{m}^3V=0.100m3. Hence,

ρ=1200.100 kg/m3\rho=\frac{120}{0.100}\,\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}^3ρ=0.100120​kg/m3
(5)
  ⟹  ρ=1200 kg/m3‾.\implies \underline{\rho = 1200\,\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}^3}.⟹ρ=1200kg/m3​.
(6)

Key assumptions¶

Fluids and especially gases comprise atoms and molecules with much space between (and within) them. We ignore these molecular level variations in mass distribution and consider most engineering fluid flows to be homogeneous.

Concept: Homogeneity

A homogeneous fluid is one for which the density is assumed to be uniform throughout the fluid, so that a single density value can be used to represent the fluid.
