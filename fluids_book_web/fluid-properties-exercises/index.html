Exercise 8 (💧)

Find the pressure relative to atmospheric pressure experienced by a diver working on the sea bed at a depth of 35 m35\,\mathsf{m}35m. Take the density of sea water to be 1030 kg/m31030\,\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}^31030kg/m3.

✓ Check your answer

353.7 kPa≈3.54 bar353.7\,\mathsf{kPa}\approx 3.54\,\mathsf{bar}353.7kPa≈3.54bar

Solution

The pressure relative to atmospheric pressure is the gauge pressure due to the liquid above the diver,

p=ρgh p=\rho g hp=ρgh

where ρ=1030 kg/m3\rho=1030\,\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}^3ρ=1030kg/m3, g=9.81 m/s2g=9.81\,\mathsf{m}/\mathsf{s}^2g=9.81m/s2 and h=35 mh=35\,\mathsf{m}h=35m. Hence,

p=1030×9.81×35 Pa p=1030\times9.81\times35\,\mathsf{Pa}p=1030×9.81×35Pa

  ⟹  p=353.7 kPa≈3.54 bar‾. \implies \underline{p=353.7\,\mathsf{kPa}\approx3.54\,\mathsf{bar}}.⟹p=353.7kPa≈3.54bar​.

Hint

To check think through your units, with pressure p=F/A  ⟹  N/m2p=F/A \implies \mathsf{N}/\mathsf{m}^2p=F/A⟹N/m2, force F=ma  ⟹  kg m/s2F=ma \implies \mathsf{kg}\,\mathsf{m}/\mathsf{s}^2F=ma⟹kgm/s2 or N\mathsf{N}N. Therefore pressure has units kg m/s2m2\mathsf{kg}\,\mathsf{m}/\mathsf{s}^2\mathsf{m}^2kgm/s2m2, simplifying to kg/m s2\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}\,\mathsf{s}^2kg/ms2 which is Pascals Pa\mathsf{Pa}Pa.

Hence,

Exercise 10 (📝)

Figure Q10: Sketch of tank with riser (not to scale)

Calculate

  1. The gauge pressure at the top of the tank.

  2. The gauge pressure at the base of the tank.

  3. The force exerted on the base of the tank due to the gauge water pressure.

Exercise 11 (📝)

A double U-tube manometer is connected to a pipe as shown in Figure Q11. Taking the dimensions and fluids as indicated, calculate the absolute pressure at point A (centre of the pipe).

Take atmospheric pressure as 1.01 bar1.01\,\mathsf{bar}1.01bar and the density of mercury as 13600 kg/m313600\,\mathsf{kg}/\mathsf{m}^313600kg/m3.

Figure Q11: Double U-tube manometer with readings, for use with exercise 11 (not to scale)

✓ Check your answer

124.7 kPa124.7\,\mathsf{kPa}124.7kPa

Solution

We start the analysis from the point where we know what the condition is, in this case atmospheric pressure on the far right of the system. We then chose horizontal planes within the same fluid where there is an interface, this is because we know that the pressure is constant since pressure is constant at any horizontal plane in the same fluid. We chose interface points as this allows us to define each point in the system as a function of the known pressure, atmospheric pressure on the far right.

Figure Q11s: Double U-tube manometer showing equilibrium level choices for solution (not to scale)

In the far right hand arm at level 1

p1=patmp_1=p_{\mathrm{atm}}p1​=patm​

and at level 2

p2=p1+ρwgh1=patm+ρwgh1…(∗)p_2=p_1+\rho_wgh_1 = p_{\mathrm{atm}}+\rho_wgh_1 \quad\dots(\ast)p2​=p1​+ρw​gh1​=patm​+ρw​gh1​…(∗).

In the second limb from right, level 2 also exists within the water layer and p2′=p2p_2'=p_2p2′​=p2​. In the second and third limbs from the right, level 2 exists in air and in both. Hence p2′′=p2′=p2p_2''=p_2'=p_2p2′′​=p2′​=p2​.

Level 3 connects the mercury in the third from right limb to the left hand limb with

p3=p2′′+ρmgh2p_3=p_2''+\rho_mgh_2p3​=p2′′​+ρm​gh2​ and

p3′=pA+ρwgh3p_3'=p_A+\rho_wgh_3p3′​=pA​+ρw​gh3​. With p3=p3′p_3=p_3'p3​=p3′​

pA=p2′′+ρmgh2−ρwgh3=p2+ρmgh2−ρwgh3p_A=p_2''+\rho_mgh_2 - \rho_wgh_3 = p_2+\rho_mgh_2 - \rho_wgh_3pA​=p2′′​+ρm​gh2​−ρw​gh3​=p2​+ρm​gh2​−ρw​gh3​.

pA=patm+g(ρwh1+ρmh2−ρwh3)p_A = p_{\mathrm{atm}}+ g(\rho_wh_1 +\rho_mh_2 - \rho_wh_3)pA​=patm​+g(ρw​h1​+ρm​h2​−ρw​h3​)
