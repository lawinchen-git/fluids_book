Measuring Pressure - Manometry

Here we consider how the principles of hydrostatic pressure can be used in devices to measure pressure.

Piezometers¶

A piezometer is a simple device for measuring moderate pressures - or differences in pressure - in a liquid (but not a gas).

The diagram above shows a pipeline containing liquid of density ρ\rhoρ. A piezometer is mounted to measure the pressure at point AAA. This consists of a thin, vertical, transparent tube with one end inserted into the top of the pipeline via a tapping, with the other end open to the atmosphere.

The elevated pressure in the pipeline liquid causes that liquid to rise up the piezometer tube to form a static column with height hAh_AhA​. The free surface of this liquid column is at atmospheric pressure.

Therefore, at the base of the liquid column the gauge pressure must be pA=ρghAp_A=\rho gh_ApA​=ρghA​.

Similarly, if a second piezometer tube is placed at tapping B, pressure in the liquid at BBB causes a static column of pipeline liquid with height hBh_BhB​ to form. The gauge pressure pBp_BpB​ at point BBB is pB=ρghBp_B=\rho gh_BpB​=ρghB​.

Together, the two piezometers measure the pressure difference pA−pBp_A-p_BpA​−pB​ between the two points

pA−pB=ρghA−ρghB=ρg(hA−hB). p_A-p_B=\rho gh_A-\rho gh_B = \rho g(h_A-h_B).pA​−pB​=ρghA​−ρghB​=ρg(hA​−hB​).

The pressure difference is determined by measuring the difference in height between the two liquid columns (hA−hBh_A-h_BhA​−hB​), given the known pipeline fluid density ρ\rhoρ.

Notes on piezometers

  • The measured pressures are gauge pressures. Because the piezometer is open to the atmosphere, the hydrostatic pressure due to the static column height will be relative to atmospheric.

  • A piezometer is open to the atmosphere and so is only suitable to measure pressures in liquids and not gasses. A gas would simply escape.

Manometers¶

A manometer shares the underlying principle of the piezometer, with the addition of a second liquid between the pipeline fluid and the atmospheric free surface. This means that a manometer can measure pressures in any fluid - liquid or gas.

The intermediary fluid is termed the “working liquid” or “manometric liquid”, with density ρm\rho_mρm​. In the configuration shown, with the working liquid sitting below the pipeline liquid, ρm>ρ\rho_m>\rhoρm​>ρ; the two are immiscible, such that an interface forms between them.

The pressure in the pipeline forces some of the fluid into the manometer tube, pushing the working fluid, such that the positions of the interfaces in each limb of the manometer shift. The pressure pAp_ApA​ at point AAA is directly related to this shift in interface levels; to be found by balancing the hydrostatic contributions of the different fluids in the manometer.

Drawing an imaginary horizontal line (XXX in the diagram) through the lower interface, we note that the pressure in the left-hand limb at this level must be the same as the pressure in the right-hand limb. This must be true for the manometer to be in equilibrium - i.e. with no flow.

From the left-hand limb, the pressure at the interface

pX=pA+ρgh1. p_X=p_A+\rho gh_1.pX​=pA​+ρgh1​.

Similarly in the right-hand limb pX=ρmgh2. p_X=\rho_mgh_2.pX​=ρm​gh2​.

Equating eliminates pXp_XpX​ and

pA=g(ρmh2−ρh1). p_A=g(\rho_mh_2-\rho h_1).pA​=g(ρm​h2​−ρh1​).

Hence the pressure pAp_ApA​ can be determined by measuring the two heights h1h_1h1​ and h2h_2h2​, for the known fluid densities ρ\rhoρ and ρm\rho_mρm​.

For example, if the pipeline above contains air with density ρ=1.2 \rho=1.2\,ρ=1.2kg/m3^33 and the working fluid is water with density ρm=1000 \rho_m=1000\,ρm​=1000kg/m3^33, with the heights measured h1=0.3 h_1=0.3\,h1​=0.3m and h2=0.1 h_2=0.1\,h2​=0.1m, we can evaluate the gauge pressure in the air

pA=g(ρmh2−ρh1)=9.81(1000×0.1−1.2×0.3)  p_A=g(\rho_mh_2-\rho h_1)=9.81(1000\times0.1-1.2\times0.3)\,pA​=g(ρm​h2​−ρh1​)=9.81(1000×0.1−1.2×0.3)Pa

  ⟹  pA=977 Pa‾. \implies \underline {p_A=977\,\text{Pa}}.⟹pA​=977Pa​.

Notes on manometers

  • The pressures above are all gauge pressures. The tube is open to the atmosphere which creates the reference pressure.

  • If the pipeline fluid is gas, the manometric liquid is typically water ρm=ρH2O=1000 \rho_m=\rho_{H_2O}=1000\,ρm​=ρH2​O​=1000kg/m3^33 or some form of oil ρm≈800\rho_m\approx 800ρm​≈800 to 900 900\,900kg/m3^33.

  • If the pipeline contains a flowing liquid, the manometric liquid is typically mercury ρm=ρHg=13600 \rho_m=\rho_{Hg}=13600\,ρm​=ρHg​=13600kg/m3^33.

  • If we have no reference pressure, we can still evaluate pressure differences using this method using a differential manometer (see worked example).

Evaluating pressures from a manometer

  1. Pressures are constant in a horizontal plane: identify the plane that exists in two arms of the manometer.

  2. Construct the hydrostatic balances in each limb, recalling that the hydrostatic pressure at the base of a water column is that at the top of the column, plus the hydrostatic contribution ρgh\rho g hρgh of the column.

  3. Apply equilibrium by equating the pressures in the two limbs at the identified plane.

Differential manometer¶

Example 6 (Differential manometer)

Two pipelines convey a gas with density ρ\rhoρ. A differential manometer with a working fluid of density ρm\rho_mρm​ measures the pressure difference pA−pBp_A-p_BpA​−pB​, between point A and point B in the respective pipelines.

The pressure difference can be found by measuring the heights h1h_1h1​ and h2h_2h2​. Show that

pA−pB=(ρm−ρ)(h1−h2)g, p_A-p_B=(\rho_m-\rho)(h_1-h_2)g,pA​−pB​=(ρm​−ρ)(h1​−h2​)g,

and hence find the pressure difference when ρ=1.2 \rho=1.2\,ρ=1.2kg/m3^33, ρm=800 \rho_m=800\,ρm​=800kg/m3^33, h1=58 h_1=58\,h1​=58mm and h2=176 h_2=176\,h2​=176mm.

Solution

We note that the horizontal plane XXX intersects each limb of the manometer and exists within one fluid; applying equilibrium at XXX in each limb will relate pAp_ApA​ and pBp_BpB​.

In the left-hand limb

pX=pA+ρgh1+ρmg(h2−h1), p_X=p_A+\rho gh_1 + \rho_m g(h_2-h_1),pX​=pA​+ρgh1​+ρm​g(h2​−h1​),

and in the right-hand limb

pX=pB+ρgh2. p_X=p_B+\rho g h_2.pX​=pB​+ρgh2​.

Equating eliminates pXp_XpX​, with

pA−pB=ρgh2−ρgh1−ρmg(h2−h1). p_A-p_B= \rho g h_2 - \rho gh_1 - \rho_m g(h_2-h_1).pA​−pB​=ρgh2​−ρgh1​−ρm​g(h2​−h1​).

Collecting terms in ρ\rhoρ and ρm\rho_mρm​, we can rearrange

pA−pB=ρg(h2−h1)−ρmg(h2−h1)=g(ρ−ρm)(h2−h1) p_A-p_B= \rho g(h_2-h_1) - \rho_m g(h_2-h_1) = g(\rho -\rho_m)(h_2-h_1)pA​−pB​=ρg(h2​−h1​)−ρm​g(h2​−h1​)=g(ρ−ρm​)(h2​−h1​)

or

pA−pB=g(ρm−ρ)(h1−h2)‾ \underline{p_A-p_B= g(\rho_m -\rho)(h_1-h_2)}pA​−pB​=g(ρm​−ρ)(h1​−h2​)​

as required.

Evaluating

Evaluating from the data provided

pA−pB=g(ρm−ρ)(h1−h2)=9.81×(800−1.2)(58−176)×10−3 p_A-p_B= g(\rho_m -\rho)(h_1-h_2) = 9.81\times(800-1.2)(58-176)\times 10^{-3}pA​−pB​=g(ρm​−ρ)(h1​−h2​)=9.81×(800−1.2)(58−176)×10−3,Pa,

  ⟹  pA−pB=−925 kPa.‾ \implies \underline{p_A-p_B=-925\,\mathsf{kPa}.}⟹pA​−pB​=−925kPa.​

Example 7 (🔥 Challenge manometer example)

A manometer consists of two tubes A and B, with vertical axes and uniform cross-sectional areas Aa=500 A_a=500\,Aa​=500mm2^22 and Ab=800 A_b=800\,Ab​=800mm2^22 respectively, connected by a U-tube C of cross-sectional area Ac=70 A_c=70\,Ac​=70mm2^22 throughout - shown in the figure below. Tube A contains a liquid of relative density Pa=0.8\Rho_a=0.8Pa​=0.8, tube B contains one of relative density Pb=0.9\Rho_b=0.9Pb​=0.9. The surface of separation between the two liquids is in the vertical side of C connected to tube A. Determine the additional pressure, ΔpB\Delta p_BΔpB​ that, when applied to tube B, will cause the surface of separation to rise Δhc=60 \Delta h_c=60\,Δhc​=60mm in tube C.

Solution

The surface in tube B moves down a distance Δhb\Delta h_bΔhb​ due to the additional pressure applied, and liquid B is pushed into tube C. The interface between the two fluids in the left hand manometer arm (“the surface of separation”) is pushed upwards due to this additional fluid.

The rise in the surface of separation will push liquid A up into tube A, raising the surface in tube A a distance Δha\Delta h_aΔha​ - shown in the diagram below.

  1. The volume change in tubes A, B and C must match each other. We can express this mathematically in terms of the height changes and the constant cross_sectional areas

AcΔhc=AbΔhb=AaΔha. A_c\Delta h_c = A_b\Delta h_b = A_a\Delta h_a.Ac​Δhc​=Ab​Δhb​=Aa​Δha​.

From here, we can find the changes in depth in each tube

Δha=AcΔhcAa,andΔhb=AcΔhcAb. \displaystyle \Delta h_a = \frac{A_c\Delta h_c}{A_a}, \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta h_b = \frac{A_c\Delta h_c}{A_b}.Δha​=Aa​Ac​Δhc​​,andΔhb​=Ab​Ac​Δhc​​.

  1. Before the extra pressure was added in tube B, the manometer was in equilibrium with the fluid depths in tubes A and B hah_aha​ and hbh_bhb​ respectively. In the left-hand arm at level 1 (horizontal plane of constant pressure existing in both arms) in the diagram

p1=pA+ρagha p_1=p_A+\rho_a gh_ap1​=pA​+ρa​gha​

and in the right-hand arm

p1=pB+ρbghb. p_1=p_B+\rho_b gh_b.p1​=pB​+ρb​ghb​.

  1. Equibibrium in the manometer after the pressure is added is given at level 2 in the diagram. In the left-hand arm

p2=pA+ρag(ha+Δha−Δhc), p_2=p_A+\rho_a g(h_a+\Delta h_a - \Delta h_c),p2​=pA​+ρa​g(ha​+Δha​−Δhc​),

and in the right-hand arm

p2=pB+ΔpB+ρbg(hb−Δhb−Δhc). p_2=p_B+\Delta p_B + \rho_b g(h_b-\Delta h_b - \Delta h_c).p2​=pB​+ΔpB​+ρb​g(hb​−Δhb​−Δhc​).

  1. Subracting the before from the after scenario in each arm can give us a pair of equations for p2−p1p_2-p_1p2​−p1​.

In the left-hand arm

p2−p1=pA+ρag(ha+Δha−Δhc)−pA−ρagha, p_2-p_1 = p_A+\rho_a g(h_a+\Delta h_a - \Delta h_c) - p_A -\rho_a gh_a,p2​−p1​=pA​+ρa​g(ha​+Δha​−Δhc​)−pA​−ρa​gha​,

which simplifies to p2−p1=ρag(Δha−Δhc). p_2-p_1=\rho_a g(\Delta h_a - \Delta h_c).p2​−p1​=ρa​g(Δha​−Δhc​).

Similarly in the right-hand arm

p2−p1=pB+ΔpB+ρbg(hb−Δhb−Δhc)−pB−ρbghb, p_2-p_1 = p_B+\Delta p_B + \rho_b g(h_b-\Delta h_b - \Delta h_c) - p_B - \rho_b gh_b,p2​−p1​=pB​+ΔpB​+ρb​g(hb​−Δhb​−Δhc​)−pB​−ρb​ghb​,

simplifying to

p2−p1=ΔpB−ρbg(Δhb+Δhc). p_2-p_1 = \Delta p_B - \rho_b g(\Delta h_b + \Delta h_c).p2​−p1​=ΔpB​−ρb​g(Δhb​+Δhc​).

Equating p2−p1p_2-p_1p2​−p1​ from the left and right-hand arms

ρag(Δha−Δhc)=ΔpB−ρbg(Δhb+Δhc), \rho_a g(\Delta h_a - \Delta h_c)= \Delta p_B - \rho_b g(\Delta h_b + \Delta h_c),ρa​g(Δha​−Δhc​)=ΔpB​−ρb​g(Δhb​+Δhc​),

which can be rewritten for the pressure change we are looking for

ΔpB=ρag(Δha−Δhc)+ρbg(Δhb+Δhc). \Delta p_B = \rho_a g(\Delta h_a - \Delta h_c) + \rho_b g(\Delta h_b + \Delta h_c).ΔpB​=ρa​g(Δha​−Δhc​)+ρb​g(Δhb​+Δhc​).

  1. From our first step, we related the changes in depth in each tube, so we can write ΔpB\Delta p_BΔpB​ now in terms of data provided

ΔpB=gρw{Pa[Δhc(AcAa−1)]+Pb[Δhc(AcAb+1)]}. \displaystyle \Delta p_B = g\rho_w\left\{\Rho_a\left[\Delta h_c\left( \frac{A_c}{A_a} - 1\right)\right] + \Rho_b\left[\Delta h_c\left( \frac{A_c}{A_b} + 1\right)\right]\right\}.ΔpB​=gρw​{Pa​[Δhc​(Aa​Ac​​−1)]+Pb​[Δhc​(Ab​Ac​​+1)]}.

  1. Evaluating from the data provided

ΔpB=9.81×1000{0.8[601000(70500−1)]+0.9[601000(70800+1)]} Pa, \displaystyle \Delta p_B = 9.81\times1000\left\{0.8\left[\frac{60}{1000}\left( \frac{70}{500} - 1\right)\right] + 0.9\left[\frac{60}{1000}\left( \frac{70}{800} + 1\right)\right]\right\}\,\mathsf{Pa},ΔpB​=9.81×1000{0.8[100060​(50070​−1)]+0.9[100060​(80070​+1)]}Pa,

  ⟹  ΔpB=172 Pa‾. \implies \underline{\Delta p_B = 172\,\mathsf{Pa}}.⟹ΔpB​=172Pa​.
